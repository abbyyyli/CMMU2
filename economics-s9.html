    Inflation and the Quantity Theory of Money通貨膨脹與貨幣數量學說

    Prices in general rise when more money chases the same goods. This chapter shows how inflation is measured, why money growth drives it, why unpredictable inflation hurts even when wages keep up, who wins and who loses, and why governments create it anyway.當更多的錢追逐同樣多的商品，物價就會普遍上漲。本章說明通膨怎麼衡量、為什麼貨幣成長是主因、為什麼就算薪水跟得上，難以預測的通膨還是有害、誰得利誰受害，以及政府為什麼還是會製造通膨。

    1. What runaway inflation looks like: Zimbabwe1. 失控的通膨長什麼樣子：辛巴威

    Around 2000, President Robert Mugabe needed money to reward allies and buy off rivals. He had already taxed almost everything, and investors had left. So he printed money.2000 年前後，總統穆加比需要錢來酬庸盟友、收買對手。他幾乎已經把能課的稅都課了，投資人也都跑了，所以他印鈔票。

    Print money to pay for spending印鈔來付支出→No new factories or productivity, so the same goods沒有新工廠或生產力，商品還是一樣多→More money chases the same goods更多錢追逐同樣的商品→Prices rise物價上漲→The government needs even more money政府需要更多錢→Print more再印更多

    That loop is why it got out of control. The faster prices rose, the more the government printed, and the faster prices rose again.這個循環就是失控的原因。物價漲得越快，政府印得越多，物價又漲得更快。

    1. ~2000Prices rising about 50% a year物價每年上漲約 50%
    2. 2001100% a year每年 100%
    3. 2002200% a year每年 200%
    4. 2003600% a year每年 600%
    5. 2006Over 1,000% a year. Z$417 for one sheet of toilet paper, not one roll每年超過 1,000%。一張衛生紙（不是一捲）要 417 辛巴威元
    6. 2008Thousands of percent a month, then a peak of 7.6 billion percent a month and Z$100 trillion notes. By the end of the year the Zimbabwe dollar is effectively dead and foreign currencies are made legal每月漲幾千 %，高峰時每月 76 億 %，印出 100 兆元面額的鈔票。年底辛巴威元等於死亡，外幣交易合法化

    People spent cash the moment they got it, because money held in the morning bought less by evening. A million Zimbabwe dollars might buy a chicken, if you were lucky. Germany in 1923, China in 1949 and Yugoslavia in 1994 went through the same thing, each time because a government desperate for cash had little to fall back on except the printing press.人們一拿到現金就馬上花掉，因為早上的錢到晚上就買得更少。一百萬辛巴威元運氣好的話能買一隻雞。1923 年的德國、1949 年的中國、1994 年的南斯拉夫都經歷過同樣的事，每一次都是政府急需現金，除了印鈔機之外幾乎沒有別的辦法。

    Hyperinflation惡性通膨 has no single official line. A common textbook threshold is 50% or more per month.惡性通膨沒有單一的官方標準。教科書常用的門檻是每月 50% 以上。

    2. Measuring inflation2. 衡量通膨

    Supply and demand push single prices up and down all the time. Think of each price as a ping pong ball bouncing inside an elevator. Inflation is the elevator going up.供需一直在把個別價格推上推下。把每個價格想成電梯裡彈跳的乒乓球，通膨就是整台電梯往上走。

    3. The quantity theory of money: M × V = P × Y3. 貨幣數量學說：M × V = P × Y

    Follow one dollar for a year. Tyler buys a pupusa from Don. Don gives the dollar to his daughter, who spends it on a pony ride. It ends up with Alex, who loses it in his couch, finds it, and buys a coffee on a road trip. One dollar, spent three times.跟著一張一元鈔票走一年。Tyler 向 Don 買了一個 pupusa；Don 把錢給女兒，女兒拿去騎小馬；最後到了 Alex 手上，他把錢掉進沙發、找回來，在公路旅行時買了一杯咖啡。同一張一元，花了三次。

    VVelocity流通速度流通速度3, since it was spent three times3，因為花了三次How many times the average dollar is spent on final goods and services in a year平均每一元一年內用來購買最終商品和服務的次數
    PPrice level物價水準The prices of the pupusa, pony ride and coffeepupusa、騎小馬和咖啡的價格The average price of all final goods and services所有最終商品和服務的平均價格
    YReal output實質產出The pupusa, pony ride and coffeepupusa、騎小馬和咖啡本身Real GDP實質 GDP
    Left side: M × V左邊：M × V

    All the money times how often it is spent. This is what buyers paid in total, which is nominal GDP.所有的錢乘上被花掉的次數，也就是買方總共付了多少錢，等於名目 GDP。

    Right side: P × Y右邊：P × Y

    Everything sold times its price. This is what sellers received in total, which is also nominal GDP.所有賣出的東西乘上價格，也就是賣方總共收了多少錢，同樣等於名目 GDP。

    Everything sold is bought by someone, so the two sides are always equal. The equation is an identity: true by definition. On its own it says nothing about cause. The theory comes in the next section, when we add facts about which letters move.每樣賣出的東西都有人買，所以兩邊永遠相等。這條式子是恆等式：依定義就成立。單看它說不出因果，理論要到下一節，加上「哪些字母會動」的事實之後才出現。

    4. What causes inflation4. 通膨的成因

    Rewrite the identity as P = M × V ÷ Y. If the price level changes, only three things can be behind it.把恆等式改寫成 P = M × V ÷ Y。物價水準若改變，背後只可能是三樣東西。

    Y: real GDPY：實質 GDP

    A 10% rise in one year would be astonishing growth. A 10% fall would be a great depression. It moves too little.一年成長 10% 已經是驚人的成長，下跌 10% 則是大蕭條。它變動太小。

    V: velocityV：流通速度

    About 7 in the US in recent years. It depends on habits like weekly or biweekly pay. It might drift to 6 or 8, usually not much more.美國近年約為 7，取決於週薪或雙週薪這類習慣。可能漂到 6 或 8，通常不會更多。

    M: moneyM：貨幣

    Can grow very fast, as in Zimbabwe or Peru. The only candidate for large, lasting price rises.可以成長得非常快，例如辛巴威或秘魯。它是大幅、持續漲價唯一可能的來源。

    When more money chases the same amount of goods and services, prices must rise.當更多的錢追逐同樣多的商品和服務，物價一定上漲。

    In growth rates, the identity becomes a rule of thumb you can use in an answer:改用成長率表示，恆等式就變成答題時可用的經驗法則：

    Inflation ≈ money growth + velocity growth − real GDP growth通膨率 ≈ 貨幣成長率 + 流通速度成長率 − 實質 GDP 成長率

    Another MRU video puts Peru's 1990 inflation at about 7,500%. The point is the same either way: money and prices rose together.另一支 MRU 影片說秘魯 1990 年通膨約 7,500%。不管哪個數字，重點都一樣：貨幣和物價一起上漲。

    Principle原則In plain words白話說
    1. Money is neutral in the long run貨幣中立1. 長期而言貨幣是中立的Double the money supply and, in time, prices double. Real output is not changed.貨幣供給加倍，一段時間後物價也加倍，實質產出不變。
    2. Inflation is a monetary phenomenon2. 通膨是貨幣現象Milton Friedman: inflation is "always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon." Large, lasting inflation always comes with fast money growth.傅利曼：通膨「無論何時何地都是一種貨幣現象」。大幅、持續的通膨一定伴隨快速的貨幣成長。
    3. Central banks control inflation3. 央行控制通膨Central banks largely control the money supply, so they largely control inflation.央行大致掌控貨幣供給，所以也大致掌控通膨。

    Reading the Thai charts together把泰國的兩張圖一起看

    The slides also show Thailand's narrow money supply, M1, from the Bank of Thailand. It rose from about 1.9 trillion baht in 2017 to about 3.6 trillion in 2026, so it nearly doubled. That is about 7% a year. Over the same years Thai inflation stayed low. The growth rule explains the gap. Real output grew, which absorbed part of the new money. Velocity fell, because people held more money relative to what they spent. So 7% money growth did not turn into 7% inflation.投影片也呈現泰國央行公布的狹義貨幣 M1：從 2017 年約 1.9 兆泰銖升到 2026 年約 3.6 兆，接近翻倍，約每年 7%。同一段時間泰國通膨一直很低。成長率法則可以解釋這個落差：實質產出成長，吸收了一部分新增的錢；流通速度下降，因為人們相對於支出持有更多的錢。所以 7% 的貨幣成長沒有變成 7% 的通膨。

    5. Why unpredictable inflation is costly5. 為什麼難以預測的通膨代價很高

    Inflation raises wages along with prices. If everyone knew it would be 2% or 8% every year, people could plan for it. The trouble is that nobody knows.通膨會讓薪資跟著物價一起漲。如果大家都知道每年是 2% 或 8%，就能事先規劃。麻煩在於沒有人知道。

    Price confusion價格混淆

    A price is a signal wrapped in an incentive. When oil gets dearer, users should economize. With inflation every price is rising, so nobody can tell whether oil is scarcer or there is simply more money around. Prices become noisy, and the economy coordinates less well.價格是包著誘因的訊號。石油變貴時，使用者應該節省。但有通膨時每個價格都在漲，沒人分得出是石油變稀少，還是只是市面上的錢變多。價格變得很吵，經濟的協調就變差。

    Money illusion貨幣幻覺

    Mistaking a change in a nominal price for a change in a real price. A movie ticket doubles while wages double too, yet movies "feel" too expensive and people go less.把名目價格的變化誤當成實質價格的變化。電影票漲一倍、薪水也漲一倍，大家卻「覺得」電影太貴，就少去看了。

    A nominal price is the number on the tag. A real price is what it costs in hours of work or other goods. A bowl of noodles going from 30 to 60 baht is no dearer in real terms if wages also doubled.名目價格是標籤上的數字。實質價格是它要花多少工時或其他商品。一碗麵從 30 泰銖漲到 60 泰銖，如果薪水也翻倍，實質上並沒有變貴。

    6. Real interest rates and arbitrary redistribution6. 實質利率與任意的財富重分配

    Loans are written in money. Inflation changes what that money is worth by the time it is repaid.貸款是用錢寫的。等到還錢時，通膨已經改變了那筆錢的價值。

    Real interest rate = nominal interest rate − inflation rate
    A bank lends $100 at 10%. Inflation is 10%. It gets back $110, which buys what $100 bought. Real return: 0.實質利率 = 名目利率 − 通膨率。銀行以 10% 借出 100 元，通膨 10%，收回的 110 元只能買到原本 100 元的東西，實質報酬為 0。

    The slides show a 1978 mortgage: a 12% nominal rate is a 2% real rate plus 10% inflation. Whether that deal turns out well depends on what inflation actually does next.投影片上是一筆 1978 年的房貸：12% 的名目利率 = 2% 實質利率 + 10% 通膨。這筆交易最後划不划算，要看之後通膨實際怎麼走。

    Surprise意外Borrowers借款人Lenders放款人MRU exampleMRU 例子
    Inflation higher than expected通膨高於預期GainLoseMortgages from the 1960s were repaid in the cheap dollars of the 1970s.1960 年代的房貸用 1970 年代變薄的美元還清。
    Inflation lower than expected通膨低於預期LoseGainA mortgage at 18% around 1981. Inflation fell from about 14% to under 5%, so the real rate became 13%.1981 年前後 18% 的房貸。通膨從約 14% 降到 5% 以下，實質利率變成 13%。

    Lenders protect themselves against the inflation they expect. If they expect 10% inflation and want a 5% real return, they charge 15%. Nominal rates rise with expected inflation: the Fisher effect費雪效果. The US chart in the slides shows it. Interest rates followed inflation up in the 1970s, peaked near 20% when inflation hit about 14%, and came down with it.放款人會針對預期的通膨保護自己。預期通膨 10%、想要 5% 的實質報酬，就收 15%。名目利率隨預期通膨上升，這就是費雪效果。投影片上的美國圖表可以看到：1970 年代利率跟著通膨上升，通膨約 14% 時利率接近 20% 的高峰，之後又跟著一起下降。

    Now make inflation high and volatile. Lenders fear a surprise rise. Borrowers fear a surprise fall. Both avoid long contracts, so 20-year mortgages and long business loans become costly and rare. Moving savings to borrowers, which is financial intermediation, starts to break down, and the economy builds less wealth.現在讓通膨又高又不穩。放款人怕通膨意外上升，借款人怕通膨意外下降，雙方都避開長約，所以 20 年房貸和長期企業貸款變得昂貴又少見。把儲蓄送到借款人手上的金融中介開始崩壞，經濟累積的財富就變少。

    中文邏輯費雪效果只保護「預期到的」通膨。財富重分配來自沒預期到的那一段：通膨比預期高，借款人賺；比預期低，放款人賺。

    The Fisher effect does not fix one problem: the government taxes nominal interest, including the part that only makes up for inflation.費雪效果解決不了一個問題：政府對名目利息課稅，連只是補償通膨的那一部分也課。

    9. Why governments create inflation9. 政府為什麼製造通膨

    If inflation is this costly, why does any government create it? MRU gives two reasons and one warning.通膨代價這麼高，為什麼還有政府要製造它？MRU 給了兩個理由和一個警告。

    Reason 1: an inflation tax通膨稅

    Printing money and spending it moves wealth from everyone holding money to the government. It is a poor tax, used when a government cannot raise money any other way, as in Zimbabwe.印鈔來花，等於把財富從所有持有貨幣的人移到政府手上。這是很差的稅，只在政府沒有其他方法籌錢時才用，例如辛巴威。

    Reason 2: a short-run boost理由 2：短期刺激

    An unexpected increase in money can raise real output for a while. That can help in a recession. It can also be abused, for example to win an election.未預期的貨幣增加可以暫時提高實質產出。在衰退時可能有幫助，但也可能被濫用，例如拿來贏選舉。

    The parable of inflation通膨寓言

    1. A government pays its soldiers with newly printed money. They buy bread. The baker thinks demand is up, works longer, hires help, bakes more and raises his price.政府用新印的鈔票付軍餉，士兵去買麵包。麵包師傅以為需求增加，延長工時、請幫手、多烤一些，也調高價格。
    2. The tailor and the carpenter feel the same, because soldiers buy from them too.裁縫和木匠也有同樣的感覺，因為士兵也向他們買東西。
    3. The baker goes to buy shirts and finds their price has risen. All three earned more dollars, but those dollars buy less. Their real wages fell.麵包師傅去買襯衫，發現襯衫也漲價了。三個人賺到更多錢，但這些錢買得更少，實質工資其實下降了。
    4. Next time the government must print more to buy the same goods. Everyone rushes to spend before prices rise, so V goes up and prices rise faster.下一次政府得印更多錢才能買到同樣的東西。大家趕著在漲價前把錢花掉，V 上升，物價漲得更快。
    5. They catch on. They expect inflation, stop working extra hours, and the boost is gone.他們學乖了，開始預期通膨，不再加班，刺激效果就消失了。

    The warning: stopping inflation is costly. Cutting money growth works the same story in reverse. Output falls until prices adjust, which is a recession. MRU compares inflation to a drug: it lifts you at first, then you need more for the same effect, then you need it just to feel normal, and quitting brings withdrawal. The US lived this. By the late 1970s inflation no longer lowered unemployment, and the country had stagflation停滯性通膨: high inflation and high unemployment together. Inflation came down in the early 1980s, under Ronald Reagan, at the price of a severe recession in 1981 and 1982.警告是：停止通膨的代價很高。減少貨幣成長會讓同一個故事倒著演：物價調整之前產出先下降，也就是衰退。MRU 把通膨比作毒品：一開始讓人振奮，接著要更多才有同樣效果，然後要用才覺得正常，戒掉時還有戒斷症狀。美國親身經歷過：到 1970 年代末，通膨已經無法再降低失業，出現停滯性通膨，高通膨和高失業同時發生。1980 年代初雷根任內通膨降下來了，代價是 1981 到 1982 年的嚴重衰退。

    中文邏輯短期印錢會讓大家「以為」生意變好；等發現什麼都漲了，就只剩物價上漲。要把通膨降回來，得先挨一段衰退。

    A natural experiment自然實驗

    Some districts got new notes quickly, so their cash shortage was short. Others waited much longer. Comparing them shows the effect of losing cash.有些地區很快拿到新鈔，現金短缺時間很短；有些地區等了很久。比較兩者就能看出失去現金的影響。

    Measuring it without good data沒有好資料時怎麼衡量

More than 99% of the old notes eventually came back into banks.發表的研究發現：受衝擊較大的地區，工作和夜間燈光減少較多、銀行放款成長較慢，也更快轉向電子錢包和卡片。全國經濟活動在 2016 年 12 月下降約 3 個百分點，之後幾個月效果逐漸消退。最後超過 99% 的舊鈔都回到了銀行。

    11. Do not mix these up11. 別搞混

    Prices doubled since 1984, so people are poorer.物價從 1984 年至今翻倍，所以大家變窮了。

    Compare real values. Wages rose more than prices on average.要比實質值。平均而言薪資漲得比物價多。

    M × V = P × Y proves money causes inflation.M × V = P × Y 證明了貨幣造成通膨。

    The identity proves nothing by itself. The theory adds that V and Y are fairly stable.恆等式本身什麼都證明不了，理論還加上了 V 和 Y 相當穩定這個事實。

    Money is never neutral.貨幣從來不是中立的。

    It is neutral in the long run. In the short run an unexpected change can move output.長期是中立的。短期內，未預期的變化可以影響產出。

    Inflation is costly because prices go up.通膨的代價就是東西變貴。

    Wages go up too. The costs come from unpredictability, transfers and broken long-term lending.薪資也會漲。代價來自難以預測、財富移轉和長期借貸崩壞。

    Inflation always hurts borrowers.通膨總是傷害借款人。

    Unexpected inflation helps borrowers. Unexpected deflation hurts them.未預期的通膨對借款人有利，未預期的通縮才傷害他們。

    If inflation is fully expected, savers are fine.只要通膨完全被預期到，存款人就沒事。

    Not when tax falls on nominal interest: 12% nominal with 9% inflation gives −1% real after tax.對名目利息課稅時就不是：名目 12%、通膨 9%，稅後實質是 −1%。

    Disinflation means prices are falling.通膨減緩就是物價在下跌。

    Disinflation means inflation is slowing. Prices still rise. Deflation is when they fall.通膨減緩是通膨變慢，物價仍在漲。物價下跌才是通縮。

    India's demonetization was too much money, like Zimbabwe.印度廢鈔和辛巴威一樣是錢太多。

    It was a sudden cut in usable cash, the reverse case.那是可用現金突然減少，是相反的情況。

    12. Cold call practice12. 課堂點名練習

    What is inflation? Give an example.什麼是通膨？舉個例子。

    Inflation is a rise in the average level of prices. If the CPI goes from 100 to 103 in a year, inflation is 3%. One good getting dearer is not inflation by itself.通膨是物價平均水準上升。CPI 一年從 100 變 103，通膨就是 3%。單一商品變貴本身不是通膨。

    What is the CPI, and why is it weighted?CPI 是什麼？為什麼要加權？

    The average price of a basket of goods and services that consumers buy. Items count by their share of spending, so housing moves it more than toothbrushes.消費者購買的一籃商品和服務的平均價格。各項目依占支出的比例計算，所以住宅對它的影響大過牙刷。

    A price index rises from 120 to 126. What is the inflation rate?物價指數從 120 升到 126，通膨率是多少？

    (126 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = 5%.(126 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = 5%。

    What are M, V, P and Y?M、V、P、Y 分別是什麼？

    The money supply, the velocity of money, the price level and real GDP. M × V is total spending and P × Y is total sales, so both equal nominal GDP.貨幣供給、貨幣流通速度、物價水準和實質 GDP。M × V 是總支出，P × Y 是總銷售，兩者都等於名目 GDP。

    What causes large, lasting inflation?大幅、持續的通膨是什麼造成的？

    Fast growth in the money supply. Real GDP and velocity move too little to explain it. Zimbabwe printed money to pay for spending and prices rose billions of percent a month.貨幣供給快速成長。實質 GDP 和流通速度變動太小，解釋不了。辛巴威印鈔來支付開銷，物價每月上漲幾十億 %。

    Money grows 8% a year, velocity is steady and real GDP grows 3%. Roughly what is inflation?貨幣每年成長 8%、流通速度不變、實質 GDP 成長 3%，通膨大約多少？

    About 5%: 8 + 0 − 3.約 5%：8 + 0 − 3。

    What does "money is neutral in the long run" mean?「長期而言貨幣是中立的」是什麼意思？

    A change in the money supply eventually changes prices only, not real output. Double the money and prices double.貨幣供給的變化最終只改變物價，不改變實質產出。錢加倍，物價就加倍。

    What is price confusion?什麼是價格混淆？

    When all prices are rising, people cannot tell whether a price rose because a good is scarcer or because of inflation, so price signals become noisy.所有價格都在漲時，人們分不出某個價格上漲是因為商品變稀少還是因為通膨，價格訊號就變得很吵。

    What is money illusion?什麼是貨幣幻覺？

    Mistaking a nominal price change for a real one, such as feeling movies are too expensive after ticket prices and wages both doubled.把名目價格變化誤當成實質變化，例如票價和薪水都翻倍後，還覺得電影太貴。

    What is the real interest rate on a 12% mortgage with 10% inflation?房貸利率 12%、通膨 10%，實質利率是多少？

    2%: the nominal rate minus inflation, as in the 1978 example from the slides.2%：名目利率減通膨，和投影片上 1978 年的例子一樣。

    Who gains from unexpected inflation?未預期的通膨讓誰得利？

    Borrowers, because they repay with money worth less. Lenders, cash savers and people on fixed incomes lose.借款人，因為他們用變薄的錢還債。放款人、持有現金的存款人和固定收入者受害。

    What is the Fisher effect?什麼是費雪效果？

    Nominal interest rates rise with expected inflation. Expecting 10% inflation and wanting a 5% real return, a lender charges 15%.名目利率隨預期通膨上升。預期通膨 10%、想要 5% 實質報酬，放款人就收 15%。

    Why does volatile inflation reduce long-term lending?為什麼不穩定的通膨會減少長期放款？

    Lenders fear a surprise rise and borrowers fear a surprise fall, so both avoid long contracts and financial intermediation breaks down.放款人怕通膨意外上升，借款人怕意外下降，雙方都避開長約，金融中介就崩壞了。

    Why can expected inflation still hurt savers?為什麼預期得到的通膨仍會傷害存款人？

    Tax is charged on nominal interest, including the part that only offsets inflation. At 12% nominal, 9% inflation and a 33% tax, the real return is −1%.稅是對名目利息課的，連只是抵銷通膨的部分也課。名目 12%、通膨 9%、稅率 33% 時，實質報酬是 −1%。

    Why do governments create inflation?政府為什麼要製造通膨？

    As a tax when they cannot raise money any other way, and for a short-run boost to output. The boost fades once people expect inflation.在沒有其他方法籌錢時當作一種稅，以及為了短期提高產出。一旦大家預期到通膨，刺激就消失。

    Why is stopping inflation costly?為什麼停止通膨代價很高？

    Cutting money growth lowers output before prices adjust, causing a recession, as in the US in 1981 and 1982.減少貨幣成長會在物價調整前先壓低產出，造成衰退，例如美國 1981 到 1982 年。

    What is stagflation?什麼是停滯性通膨？

    High inflation and high unemployment at the same time, as in the US in the late 1970s.高通膨和高失業同時出現，例如 1970 年代末的美國。

    Why is India's demonetization called a natural experiment?為什麼印度廢鈔被稱為自然實驗？

    Districts got new notes at different speeds by chance, so researchers could compare places with short and long cash shortages.各地區拿到新鈔的速度不同，而且出於偶然，研究者因此能比較現金短缺時間長短不同的地方。

    "Wages rise with prices, so the main problem is unpredictability. High, volatile inflation blurs price signals, moves wealth between borrowers and lenders, and makes long-term lending too risky."「薪資會跟著物價漲，所以主要問題是難以預測。又高又不穩的通膨會模糊價格訊號、在借貸雙方之間移轉財富，並讓長期放款風險太高。」

    Who wins and who loses誰贏誰輸

    "When inflation is higher than expected, borrowers gain because they repay in cheaper money. Lenders, cash savers and people on fixed incomes lose. When inflation is lower than expected, it is the reverse."「通膨高於預期時，借款人用變薄的錢還債而得利，放款人、持有現金的存款人和固定收入者受害。通膨低於預期時則相反。」

    Why governments do it政府為什麼這樣做

    "Governments use inflation as a tax when they cannot raise money any other way, or to boost output in the short run. The boost fades once people expect inflation, and bringing inflation down again usually causes a recession."「政府在沒有其他方法籌錢時把通膨當成稅，或用它在短期提高產出。一旦大家預期到通膨，刺激就消退，而要再把通膨降下來通常會造成衰退。」
