    Strategic Marketing Management · Session 5

    STP, Competition & Marketing Ethics

    A brand must stand for something, but customers can choose across brands and categories. Start with needs and occasions, test competitors and substitutes, and check incremental profit without losing customer trust.

    STP: strategic focusCompetition: alternativesProfit and ethics: reality check

    5a · STP

    Whose needs matter? Which segments are worth pursuing? What should the brand mean?

    5b · Competition

    Will behavior change, will the business earn incremental profit, and is the offer fair?

    1. Segmentation · Start with meaningful differences

    Segmentation divides a diverse market into groups that share needs, characteristics or usage conditions. The challenge is choosing a difference that actually affects value or behavior.

    中文邏輯分群要依「需求和使用情境」的差異，不是只看年齡性別；同一個人也可能跨群、買多個品牌。

    Geographic

    Country, region, access, climate; where the offer is feasible.

    Demographic

    Age, income, family stage; describes who, not necessarily why.

    Psychographic 心理特徵

    Motives, interests, values and lifestyle; the reason benefits may matter.

    Behavioral

    Occasion, frequency, brand repertoire, benefit sought and switching.

    Four tests for an effective segment

    MeasurableCan you size or describe the opportunity?
    AccessibleCan your channels and message reach it?
    SubstantialIs demand large and valuable enough?
    ActionableCan you serve it with a viable offer?

    A platform reporting 500,000 people with an interest does not show that 500,000 will buy. Similarly, the lecturer's left-handed-consumer example shows that a countable group is not automatically a relevant market for every product.

    Decision lab · Segment before you target

    Would you launch a special product for this group?

    Change the proposed segment. Identify which unanswered question could invalidate the business case.

    Age is a description, not a need.

    Research the benefit, purchasing occasion and substitutes; age alone does not establish a separate product requirement.

    Pause and answer aloud

    Why can a large measurable segment still be a poor target market? Give an example of a better behavioral variable.

    2. Targeting · More variants can mean less profit

    Targeting chooses segments the company can reach, satisfy and serve sustainably. No strategy is automatically best: balance coverage, specialization, costs and competitive response.

    中文邏輯多推一個口味不等於多賺：要扣掉自家產品被搶走的銷量和新增成本，看的是增量利潤。

    Undifferentiated

    One broad offer for many buyers; simpler to produce and communicate, but may be too generic.

    Candy / basic gum
    Differentiated

    Multiple products or messages for different benefits; potentially higher sales, complexity and cannibalization 自家互搶.

    Breeze / Omo / shampoo lines
    Concentrated / niche 利基

    Deep focus on a small group; strong fit, but limited customer pool and purchase frequency.

    Ryka pool shoes / Jolt Cola

    Product line proliferation · Sales are not incremental profit

    Variant launches can produce trial and fill shelf space, yet some purchases simply move from an existing company brand. The lecturer uses Breeze versions, shampoo variants, unusual Pepsi flavors and Lay's/Pringles choices to ask whether added SKUs truly create demand.

    Interactive · Cannibalization economics

    What did the company actually gain?

    Hypothetical launch. The new product's margin is counted on every new sale, but existing contribution lost from diverted sales must be deducted.

    Gross new SKU units1,000
    Truly additional units (assumed)300
    Incremental operating contribution+THB 10,000

    A specialty niche has a related problem: even if every customer is interested, potential buyers × buying frequency may be too small. Being a highly distinctive product is not proof of commercial viability.

    A Taste of Marketing · Repertoire is not the absence of preference

    Single-month observation

    A customer visits one Indian restaurant exactly once. That outlet has 100% of their observed Indian-restaurant visits that month.

    What the observation cannot prove

    Permanent loyalty, no future switching, or an exclusive consumer segment. Test a longer period and other occasions.

    The lecturer points out that the same individual can use an Apple and a Samsung device, or own a Volvo and a sports car. One person can belong to several apparent psychographic profiles. A target does not exclude other buyers.

    Pause and answer aloud

    Why might differentiated marketing increase total sales but reduce net profit? How does observed share over one month differ from customer loyalty?

    3. Positioning · What the customer actually thinks

    Positioning is the place a brand occupies in consumers' minds relative to alternatives. A firm's intended slogan is not the same as the associations that shoppers actually form.

    中文邏輯定位由顧客心中的印象決定，不是公司寫的標語；4Ps 要一致，否則就是定位混亂。

    Find a meaningful advantage→Select a credible promise→Deliver with the 4Ps→Research the perceived result
    FailureWhat it looks likeClass illustration
    Underpositioning 定位不足Consumers recognize the name but cannot say what the brand stands for.Weak or fuzzy associations.
    Overpositioning 定位過窄The promise is so narrow that other relevant buyers or uses feel excluded.Cooler Club's gendered message in the lecturer's discussion.
    Confused positioning 定位混亂Product, price, placement and communication send different signals.Lux: celebrity luxury image versus low shelf placement and discounting in the class anecdote.

    Meaningful differentiation can come from the product, service, employees or recognizable brand assets. A scientific-sounding reason to believe (RTB) 理由支撐 or a "No. 1" claim is not proof that customers value the advantage. Always ask No. 1 in which defined market and measured how?

    5. Disintermediation · Cutting out the middleman is not free

    Disintermediation 去中間化 bypasses intermediaries. Vertical integration 垂直整合 brings an activity under ownership or control; the two can overlap, but are not synonyms.

    中文邏輯去掉中間商，中間商做的倉儲、配送、售後工作不會消失，只是變成自己的成本。

    Manufacturer→Wholesaler→Retailer→Customer
    Manufacturer→Direct web / delivery→Customer

    Direct sales may improve customer relationships and pricing control, but the producer must now handle acquisition, fulfillment, service, returns and possible retailer conflict. The middleman's markup is not pure free profit.

    6. Direct vs. indirect competition · Define the category first

    In the lecturer's category-based definition, direct competitors sell the same product/service type. Indirect competitors come from another category but can satisfy the same need, occasion, budget or time constraint.

    中文邏輯先用品類定義分直接或間接競爭，再從顧客「同一個場合、同一筆預算」看真正的替代品。

    Direct competition

    Coke versus Pepsi; one coffee brand versus another; one pretzel seller versus another.

    Indirect competition

    Auntie Anne's versus a Dairy Queen snack option; sports drinks versus water; mouth strips versus chewing gum.

    Case · Auntie Anne's vs. Dairy Queen

    The lecturer describes research in Thailand suggesting that a pretzel stand's practical rival could be a different-category snack sold at a comparable price in the same mall. The customer asks "What can I eat here and now with this budget?" rather than "Which pretzel brand exists?" This is a class research anecdote, not a current national market-share claim.

    Consumer job→Alternative offers→Realistic same-occasion substitution→Direct / indirect label

    Classification always depends on the market definition. The lecturer treats BTS and MRT as similar types of urban rail transport, whereas rail versus taxi is a different transport mode. A product can be indirect by category yet a powerful practical rival.

    Customer Value Analysis · Benefits and costs from the buyer's view

    Value is a comparison of perceived benefits and sacrifices: money, time, effort, risk and social meaning. It is not merely the cheapest option, nor proof that consumers always optimize rationally. Competitive intelligence should reduce decision uncertainty without waiting forever for perfect data.

    Microwave-popcorn lesson: before estimating Thai demand for microwave popcorn, ask how many accessible households have a usable microwave. A large population is not a large addressable market when a necessary condition is missing.
    Pause and answer aloud

    Could a product outside your official category take the customer's purchase? Give the category, the common job, and the evidence you would collect.

    7. Competitive growth · New users, new uses, more usage

    The lecturer uses these three paths as questions, not promises. Each path requires a realistic source of demand and an ethical check.

    New users

    Increase category penetration 品類滲透率 by converting non-users. The potential depends on current penetration and access.

    Floor cleanerShampoo
    New uses

    Find additional valuable uses or formats. Customers may discover uses before marketers do.

    More usage

    Growth examples and caveats

    CaseGrowth mechanismQuestion worth asking
    Baking sodaFrom baking to deodorizing refrigerators, freezer spaces, carpets and litter boxes.Do the benefits create additional repeat occasions?
    Utaitip → UtipConsumer-discovered lip/cheek use inspired a distinct cosmetic format, as recounted in class.Does the new product meet the usage and regulatory context?
    Toothpaste / shampooPackaging or advertising might encourage using more per occasion.Is additional usage useful or wasteful?

    Innovation vs. planned obsolescence

    Gillette's moves from single to multiple blades illustrate new features, consumable refills, copycat competition and the possibility of higher prices. More blades or a scientific-sounding enhancement do not necessarily increase value for everyone.

    Useful innovation

    A demonstrable performance, durability, service or convenience improvement that helps relevant buyers.

    Planned obsolescence

    Deliberately engineering premature replacement. This can generate turnover at the expense of consumer welfare and trust.

    The lecturer also discusses The Best a Man Can Get as broad advertising puffery, distinct from a specific factual claim requiring evidence. Health-related repurposing anecdotes in the lecture are not medical advice.

    8. Downsizing · Same shelf price, less product

    Downsizing (shrinkflation) reduces net quantity without a proportional price reduction. The shelf label can stay unchanged while the unit price rises.

    中文邏輯同價縮量：數量減少的百分比用舊容量當分母，單位價格上漲的百分比會更大。

    Interactive · Unit price calculator

    Quantity reduction is not the same % as unit-price increase

    The 355, 330 and 320 mL examples come from lecture discussions; the sample shelf price is hypothetical.

    Quantity change−9.86%
    Unit-price change+10.94%
    New price per 100 mLTHB 9.38

    What shoppers should check

    Actual net weight or mL, unit price, package count and date/variant. Similar-looking boxes can hold different amounts.

    What managers should check

    Consumer disclosure, complaint rates, repeat behavior, unit margin, reputation and whether the portion serves a real need.

    The lecturer's packaging comparisons include beer/can formats, Nestlé versus differently sized packs (such as 800 versus 830 mL), bottled water and snack packages. A photograph or similar package silhouette alone cannot establish today's net amount: compare a dated, labeled product with the same variant and market.

    Not every smaller package is unethical. Clearly disclosed smaller portions may have legitimate convenience or waste-reduction benefits. The lecture's concern is concealed quantity changes that mislead buyers about value. Cost savings alone do not prove higher net profit.

    Pause and answer aloud

    A package shrinks from 355 to 320 mL at an unchanged price. Which denominator belongs in the quantity-reduction calculation, and why is unit-price inflation a different percentage?

    10. Cold call & integrated case practice

    Answer aloud in English first, then open the answer. These are exam-ready formulations grounded in the supplied Review note, not direct lecturer quotations.

    What is STP?

    Segmentation divides the market by relevant differences, targeting selects attractive and feasible groups, and positioning builds the intended brand meaning relative to alternatives.

    Why does the professor criticize strict STP?

    One person can buy across brands, categories and apparent segments. Segments guide decisions but do not dictate behavior.

    What makes segmentation effective?

    Measurability, accessibility, substantiality and actionability.

    What is a niche's biggest risk?

    A specialized group may be too small or buy too infrequently; rivals can divide the limited demand further.

    What is cannibalization?

    A firm's new product takes sales from its existing products instead of attracting genuinely additional purchases.

    What is positioning?

    The meaning or place a brand occupies in consumers' minds relative to competitors. Managers can intend a position, but consumers form the actual perception.

    What is differentiation?

    A meaningful, credible distinction that customers value and that the company can deliver. Difference alone is not enough.

    How do you create one?

    Define the consumer occasion, select meaningful axes, label the directions, plot relevant brands using evidence or explicitly marked hypotheses, and interpret the alternatives.

    What is underpositioning?

    Consumers do not have a sufficiently clear association with the brand.

    What is overpositioning?

    A brand is perceived too narrowly, potentially excluding other relevant users or occasions.

    What is confused positioning?

    Customers receive inconsistent messages from communication, pricing, distribution or actual experience.

    Can an empty map area be an opportunity?

    Possibly, but it may also represent a combination customers do not want. Validate demand and economics first.

    Direct vs. indirect competition?

    Direct competitors belong to the same product/service category; indirect competitors come from different categories but can substitute for a similar need or occasion.

    Why is indirect competition important?

    Customers choose based on needs, money, time and availability, not always the category definitions managers use.

    What is disintermediation?

    Removing intermediaries between producers and users, often by selling or delivering directly.

    Disintermediation vs. vertical integration?

    Disintermediation removes a stage; integration takes control of a stage. They may overlap but are not identical.

    Why can D2C fail?

    A producer may underestimate fulfillment, acquisition and service costs or provoke conflict with existing retailers.

    What are entry and exit barriers?

    Entry barriers make it hard to start competing; exit barriers make it costly or difficult to leave, such as long-term service obligations.

    What are three consumption-growth strategies?

    Attract new category users, find new uses or occasions, and increase the volume or frequency of use.

    What is planned obsolescence?

    Deliberately designing products to wear out or become replaceable sooner; this differs from useful innovation and raises ethical concerns.

    What is downsizing?

    Reducing net quantity without a proportionate reduction in selling price, increasing the effective unit price.

    Why is hidden downsizing problematic?

    Consumers may misjudge value if package size changes are concealed; trust and future repeat purchases may suffer.

    Why is revenue not enough?

    Sales can shift across the firm's products or be generated through high costs. We must evaluate truly incremental contribution and profit.

    Integrated mini-case · New drink for Bangkok gym-goers

    Fictional scenario: A startup wants to sell lightly flavored water to young gym-goers. It cites 400,000 social-media interests as proven buyers, claims to have no competitors because its flavor is unique, and plans to position as premium while offering heavy discounts. It also considers shrinking the pack later without highlighting the change.

    Decision→Concept→Case evidence→Mechanism→Trade-off→Test & metric
    What is wrong with using 400,000 interests as demand?

    Interests are an audience estimate, not proof of willingness to pay, category frequency, retail availability or repeat behavior. Qualify a real segment and test trial and retention with representative research.

    Who are the direct and indirect competitors?

    Direct competitors may include other flavored hydration or sports-water products in the defined category. Indirect competitors could include bottled water, fruit juice or other refreshments that serve the same post-exercise occasion. Confirm substitution with actual buyer evidence.

    Does premium positioning fit heavy discounting?

    It may produce confused signals if price, product, packaging, retail environment and advertising conflict. Research perceived value and use a coherent pricing strategy, while recognizing that not all temporary promotions necessarily damage a premium brand.

    What should the manager decide about undisclosed downsizing?

    Reject concealed reductions. Price and quantity changes should be transparent, with clear net-content disclosure and realistic assessment of long-term customer trust, repeat purchase and profitability.

    Final decision framework

    Customer + occasion→Meaningful segment→Credible position→Direct + indirect rivals→Incremental profit→Fair, testable decision
