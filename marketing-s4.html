    Case · Tops: posters are not brand experience

    A company can display friendly-service values everywhere in its office yet deliver an indifferent store greeting. Customers judge the actual touchpoint, not only the internal slogan.

    Market-oriented mission and brand fit

    “We sell photocopiers” is narrower than “We help offices work productively.” The first depends on today's technology; the second centers on an enduring customer need.

    Class exampleBusiness questionWhat not to assume
    Malee · “The Fruit Expert”Would a mushroom drink fit the brand promise?Brand extension is not automatically good or bad.
    StarbucksDoes it sell only coffee, or also a third place and social experience?The product is not always the entire value.
    AT&T / XeroxCommunications and office productivity vs. specific hardware?Broad missions still need capabilities.
    Family businessCan founder values survive delegation and succession?Family generation alone does not predict failure.

    Mission ≠ slogan. A useful mission changes which opportunities a firm pursues and what customers actually experience.

    2. BCG: classify the business, then judge the option

    A portfolio uses limited resources across products or business units. BCG compares market growth rate and relative market share — not total sales and not profitability itself.

    Star

    Practice cutoffs only: 10% annual growth and 1.0× relative share. They are not universal BCG rules or real market data. The dot uses stronger share on the left, the conventional BCG orientation.

    Product Life Cycle · different stages, different management questions

    A product category may move through introduction, growth, maturity and decline. Early growth often requires awareness and distribution investment. Mature markets may place greater weight on repeat purchase, efficiency and differentiation. Decline can call for renewal, repositioning or withdrawal — but the decision still requires evidence.

    PLC asks: what stage?

    The Product Life Cycle describes how a category's sales may develop over time. It is a useful pattern, not a guaranteed timetable.

    BCG asks: what position?

    The matrix combines market growth with our relative competitive share. A mature category is not automatically a Cash Cow for every brand.

    中文邏輯PLC 描述市場或產品可能經歷的生命週期；BCG 還要看「本公司的相對市占率」。市場成熟不代表每個品牌都成了金牛事業。

    BCG decision check · A Star is losing money. Must we invest?

    Not automatically. First check whether losses come from temporary growth investment or an unviable business model. Compare future margins, funding needs, competitive advantage and alternatives. Quadrant → hypotheses → evidence → decision, not quadrant → command.

    SWOT and environmental scanning

    Internal: Strengths / Weaknesses

    Resources, capabilities, execution and constraints inside the firm.

    External: Opportunities / Threats

    Competition, technology, demographics, politics, culture and changing customer needs.

    Generational cohort labels often come from a specific national history. Do not copy U.S. Gen X/Y meanings directly into Thailand without evidence of comparable formative experiences.

    Kodak illustrates why a business defined too narrowly by current film technology can be vulnerable when consumers continue wanting memories but adopt a different way to capture and share them.

    SWOT ≠ strategy. Turning a box into a decision requires an objective, a realistic action and evidence that it will create value.

    4. Buyer Decision Process: a guide, not a compulsory ritual

    Marketing stimuli pass through the consumer's “black box”: needs, experience, social context and perception shape what the customer eventually does.

    Interactive · How the five stages change

    Switch situations. Select a stage to see why it is long, brief or sometimes skipped.

    These are plausible paths, not fixed empirical stage durations. The professor's Volvo anecdote illustrates an exception; it does not mean all car buying is impulsive.

    Who is buying? Demographics, psychographics and self-image

    Demographics

    Age, family status, income, location: who the buyer is.

    Psychographics

    Values, interests, lifestyle, identity: why the benefit matters.

    Corvette and station-wagon image exercises show that a brand can project a personality or ideal self-image without proving the real age, wealth or family situation of all its owners.

    TrapVALS and other segments organize hypotheses; they do not reveal each person's entire identity. 品牌印象 ≠ 真實買家的人口統計。

    中文抓重點人口統計能描述「誰」，但無法單靠年齡預測「為什麼買」。教授用跑車與旅行車讓你看到品牌形象和真實車主可能完全不同。

    5. Motivation, involvement, risk and postpurchase behavior

    The same category can satisfy physiological, safety, belonging, esteem and self-actualization motives in different proportions.

    Need / context→Importance & perceived risk→Information search→Choice→Postpurchase feeling
    Do not mix upWhat it means
    InvolvementHow important or risky the decision seems before/during purchase. Price alone does not determine it.
    Perceived riskExpected consequences: financial, functional, social or safety concerns.
    Cognitive dissonanceUncomfortable doubt after choosing between appealing alternatives.
    DiffusionHow an innovation spreads across adopters with different risk and benefit perceptions.

    Water for instant coffee vs. water used for infant formula: the purpose changes perceived stakes and therefore involvement. This is about the shopper's perception, not medical advice or a claim that one water is unsafe.

    Louis Vuitton vs. Hermès: someone may like the item purchased yet wonder if the forgone option was better. That is postpurchase dissonance, not necessarily dissatisfaction.

    Microwave vs. CD adoption: the perceived advantage, risk, compatibility and ease of use can affect adoption pace. An innovator buys early; an opinion leader influences others. One does not guarantee the other.

    Maslow's needs · several motives can operate at once

    Maslow's categories include physiological needs, safety, belonging, esteem and self-actualization. These are useful prompts for exploring motivation; the professor emphasizes that they need not activate one at a time in a strict ladder.

    In a restaurant, food may satisfy hunger, safe preparation addresses security, eating with friends supports belonging, and choosing a premium venue may express esteem. Which motive is most important depends on the customer and occasion — this is a question for marketing research, not a fact the framework can answer alone.

    中文邏輯同一餐可能同時滿足吃飽、安全、社交與面子需求。不要把 Maslow 當成「第一層完全滿足才能進入第二層」的固定規則。

    Diffusion of Innovations · early buyer vs. influential buyer

    Innovators may accept uncertainty to try new technology early; other buyers may wait for reliability, a larger product ecosystem or lower prices. The professor's software-upgrade and new media-format stories highlight compatibility risk and waiting costs, not a universal adoption schedule.

    Innovator ≠ Opinion Leader. Someone who buys first does not necessarily persuade others. To market a new product, ask both who will try it and whose recommendation other customers trust.

    6. Coffee choice: which benefit matters in this situation?

    Utilitarian benefits 功能性價值, hedonic/social benefits 情感／體驗價值, taste and convenience can all coexist. Their importance changes with the purchase occasion.

    Interactive · Change the coffee context

    A teaching model of relative decision weights — not measured preferences from class research.

    The numbers are illustrative weights summing to 100, not claims about Thai consumers or a particular brand. Test the hypothesis in Session 3 research.

    Creative message vs. measurable marketing outcome

    In class, the Xcite mint example shows how a memorable creative concept can attract attention but fail to connect clearly to the brand. The same goes for premium and private-label bottled water: perceived cleanliness, convenience, taste, trust, status and packaging matter differently by customer and occasion.

    Attention ≠ brand recall ≠ consideration ≠ purchase ≠ profit. Pick the metric that matches the objective.

    7. Brand awareness is not the same as consideration or purchase

    This class distinguishes brands the consumer knows (the lecturer's use of “evoked set”) from brands they would actually consider, and then from brands purchased. Other textbooks sometimes define “evoked set” more narrowly.

    Interactive · Brand-choice funnel

    Start with 100 potential shoppers. Change what share knows the brand, what share of the aware would consider it, and what share of considerers purchase.

    These are example counts and conditional conversion rates. The sequence is for reasoning; slider changes do not prove any marketing action caused an outcome.

    中文邏輯分析水品牌，不是「水明明差不多，為何有人買貴？」而是先區分不同顧客、用途、風險、情緒，再找要用什麼指標驗證。

    Decision practice · what would convince you?

    A premium water bottle and a cheaper private-label bottle can both meet the basic need for hydration. But the buying occasion changes the perceived trade-off: convenience at home, reassurance when caring for a child, or appearance at a public event. The professor uses these contrasts to reveal consumer motives, not to prove that expensive water is medically superior.

    Case A · A consumer says: “I buy premium water because it tastes better.” What can we conclude?

    Evidence: the consumer reports a taste preference. Not yet proven: that taste objectively explains the premium purchase or predicts other consumers. A blind taste test and an occasion-specific choice study could help distinguish sensory preference from branding and social context.

    Case B · A bottled-water advertisement gets lots of views. Did the campaign succeed?

    It depends on the objective. Views may support an awareness goal, but cannot by themselves prove brand attribution, consideration or sales. Choose the outcome first, then the relevant metric; compare results with a suitable baseline if making a causal claim.

    10. Cold call & Class Discussion practice

    Try saying the answer in English before opening it. These responses are short practice scripts, not recorded quotations.

    What makes a mission different from an objective?

    A mission explains whom the organization serves and the value it aims to create. Objectives turn that direction into measurable results, deadlines and actions.

    Why doesn't a Star automatically deserve more investment?

    BCG describes growth and relative market position, but investment still depends on profitability, cash needs, capabilities and feasible future returns.

    What is Ansoff's diversification strategy?

    Entering a new market with a new product. It differs from product development, where the market already exists, and market development, where the product already exists.

    Why can a car buyer skip stages in the Buyer Decision Process?

    The five stages are a conceptual model. Prior experience, urgency, emotion or an impulse can shorten search or evaluation even in a high-priced category.

    What is the difference between demographics and psychographics?

    Demographics describe characteristics such as age or income. Psychographics examine values, lifestyle and motives that may better explain choices.

    Why is postpurchase dissonance different from dissatisfaction?

    A buyer may like a product while wondering whether a forgone alternative would have been better. Dissatisfaction is usually about perceived poor product or service performance.

    How should a marketer analyze premium bottled water?

    I would define the segment and buying occasion, identify the important functional, risk-related and symbolic benefits, then test which message improves consideration or trial rather than assuming premium means healthier.

    What was the marketing weakness of the Xcite creative example?

    A memorable idea may not link to the advertised brand. We should measure brand attribution, recall and relevant consumer response, not just attention.

    Why can't we copy U.S. generational cohorts directly into Thailand?

    The experiences defining a cohort depend on local history, culture and technology. Birth-year labels alone do not establish the same motives or behavior in a different market.
